Europe: Countries and Capitals

Europe packs dozens of countries into a small area, which makes it one of the toughest maps in GEO-SPACERACE. This guide covers the countries most often included in Europe and their capitals, plus a few facts that come up again and again in trivia.

Countries and capitals

Lists of European countries differ slightly. Russia, Turkey, Kazakhstan and the countries of the Caucasus stretch across both Europe and Asia and are sometimes included. Kosovo is recognized as independent by many but not all countries. The table below covers the countries most commonly counted as European.

Capitals with a twist

  • The Netherlands: Amsterdam is the official capital, but the government, parliament and royal working palace are in The Hague.
  • Switzerland: Bern is the seat of government, but the largest city is Zürich and the best-known international city is Geneva.
  • Same name as the country: Luxembourg, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City all share a name with their capital.
  • Cyprus: its capital, Nicosia, has been divided since 1974.

Europe by the numbers

  • Smallest country: Vatican City, which is smaller than half a square kilometer and sits entirely inside the city of Rome.
  • Largest country entirely in Europe: Ukraine. Russia is bigger, but most of its land is in Asia.
  • The microstates: Andorra, Liechtenstein, Malta, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City. They are easy to miss on the map, so zoom in on the Pyrenees, the Alps, Italy and the Mediterranean.
  • Doubly landlocked: Liechtenstein is surrounded only by other landlocked countries (Switzerland and Austria), so you would have to cross at least two borders to reach the sea.

Tips for the Europe map

  1. Learn the big anchors first: Spain, France, Germany, Italy, Poland and Ukraine. Everything else fits around them.
  2. Then the Balkans: Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, Kosovo, North Macedonia and Albania are the most-missed group. Practice them from north to south.
  3. Remember the Baltic states in order: Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania run from north to south, in alphabetical order.
