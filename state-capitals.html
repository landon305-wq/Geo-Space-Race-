Some capitals are the largest city in their state, which makes them easier: Phoenix, Denver, Atlanta, Boston, Indianapolis, Columbus, Nashville, Oklahoma City, Salt Lake City and Honolulu are all examples.

Fun capital facts

  • Phoenix is the most populous state capital in the country.
  • Montpelier, Vermont is the smallest state capital by population, with fewer than 10,000 residents.
  • Juneau, Alaska cannot be reached by road from the rest of North America. Visitors arrive by plane or boat.
  • Two capitals contain their state's name: Indianapolis and Oklahoma City.
  • Columbia (South Carolina) and Columbus (Ohio) are different cities, and both are capitals. Mixing them up is one of the most common mistakes.
  • Saint Paul, not Minneapolis, is the capital of Minnesota. The two cities sit right next to each other and are called the Twin Cities.

Memory tricks

  • Group by region. Learn the capitals one region at a time. Our U.S. regions guide gives you the groups.
  • Make silly links. "Pierre is a pier in South Dakota." "Boise is for the boys of Idaho." The sillier the picture, the better it sticks.
  • Focus on the 14 tricky ones above. If you know which capitals are not the big city, you will avoid most mistakes.
