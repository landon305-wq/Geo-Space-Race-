State Nicknames and Where They Come From

Every state has at least one nickname, and many trivia questions use them as clues. Some nicknames are official, set by state law, and others are simply popular. Here is the best-known nickname for each state, with a short explanation of where it comes from.

Nickname patterns that help in trivia

  • Named after nature: many nicknames come from a plant or animal, such as the Sunflower State, the Magnolia State, the Pelican State or the Beaver State. If a clue mentions a state flower or bird, think of these.
  • Named after history: the First State, the Centennial State, the Constitution State and the Volunteer State all point to moments in American history.
  • Named after land: the Granite State, the Green Mountain State, the Mountain State and the Great Lakes State describe the land itself.
  • Named after natural resources: the Silver State, the Treasure State and the Gem State all come from mining.

Nicknames are a good way to learn something about every state while you study the map. Combine them with our state capitals guide for even more practice.
