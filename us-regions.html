The 50 States by Region

Thinking about the United States in regions is the fastest way to get better at map trivia. Instead of trying to remember 50 separate states, you learn a handful of groups, and then the states inside each group.

The official regions

There is no single "correct" way to divide the country, but the most widely used system comes from the U.S. Census Bureau. It splits the 50 states and Washington, D.C. into 4 regions and 9 divisions. Government statistics, news reports and many trivia questions use these groupings.

Surprises worth remembering

  • Delaware, Maryland and D.C. count as the South in the Census system, even though many people think of them as Mid-Atlantic or Northeast.
  • Oklahoma and Texas are in the South, not the West. They share a division with Arkansas and Louisiana.
  • Missouri is Midwestern in the Census system, although it borders eight states and has strong Southern ties in its southern half.
  • Alaska and Hawaii are both Pacific states, grouped with California, Oregon and Washington.
  • Pennsylvania is in the Northeast even though its western half, around Pittsburgh, feels closer to the Midwest in culture and geography.

Other common groupings

Trivia questions often use informal regions too. These are not official, so their exact borders vary, but the core states are well known:

  • New England: the six states of the Census division above. This one is unusually precise and almost always means exactly those six.
  • The Great Lakes states: the eight states that touch at least one Great Lake: Minnesota, Wisconsin, Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania and New York.
  • The Gulf Coast: the five states on the Gulf of Mexico: Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama and Florida.
  • The Four Corners: Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico and Utah, the only place where four states meet at a single point.
  • The Pacific Northwest: usually Washington and Oregon, often with Idaho.

Practice tips

  1. Learn the borders between regions first. If you know the Mississippi River runs between many Midwestern and Southern states, placing the states on either side gets much easier.
  2. Count as you go. When a question covers a whole region, count the states you have clicked. If the Northeast has 9 and you have found 8, you know exactly how many are left.
  3. Don't forget the small ones. Rhode Island, Delaware, Connecticut and New Jersey are easy to miss on a crowded map. Zoom in on the East Coast before you finish.
